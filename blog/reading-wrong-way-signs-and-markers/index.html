Red reflectors, WRONG WAY signs, and arrows: how the road tells you to stop

Nino Mihilli· October 9, 2026 ·4 min read

Most drivers have passed thousands of wrong-way warnings without noticing them. That is by design. The devices are built to be invisible to people going the right way and impossible to miss for people going the wrong way.

Knowing what they look like is useful anyway. If you ever see one facing you, the right response is to stop, and the sooner you recognize it, the more time you have.

DO NOT ENTER and WRONG WAY signs

These are the two signs most associated with wrong-way prevention, and they work as a pair.

The DO NOT ENTER sign is a red circle with a white horizontal bar and white lettering. It sits where a driver could turn onto a one-way road or into the bottom of an exit ramp, facing that driver.

The WRONG WAY sign is a red rectangle with white lettering. It is typically placed farther along the ramp, as a second warning to a driver who has already passed the first.

If you see either one facing you, you are pointed against traffic. Stop as soon as you safely can.

Why some signs are bigger and lower

Standard sign heights are set for drivers scanning the road normally. A driver who is disoriented, impaired, or simply looking down at a phone may not be looking at that height.

Arizona's response, described on ADOT's wrong-way driving page, has included installing larger and lowered "Wrong Way" and "Do Not Enter" signs on freeway ramps. A lower sign sits closer to where headlights shine and closer to a driver's line of sight, which makes it harder to miss.

Arrows and red reflectors

On many off-ramps, there are large white arrows on the pavement pointing in the correct direction of travel. To a driver going the right way, they are just more lane markings.

Paired with those arrows are reflectors that do something clever. ADOT describes them as reflectors that glow bright red when a vehicle is traveling the wrong way. A driver headed the correct direction sees them as ordinary; a driver headed the wrong way sees a line of red lights in their headlights.

That is one of the most useful cues to remember. Red reflectors on the pavement in front of you mean you are going the wrong way. On a normal road at night, you should not see red reflective markers facing you on the lane you are driving in.

Signs that light up

Some ramps have signs with flashing LED lights around them. In Arizona, the thermal-camera detection system on I-17 triggers internally illuminated "Wrong Way" signs with flashing red lights when it detects a wrong-way vehicle, while also alerting the Arizona Department of Public Safety and posting warnings on overhead message boards for other drivers.

If a sign suddenly starts flashing at you, it may be responding to your vehicle. Stop.

Do these measures work?

The evidence suggests they do, especially in combination.

A brief from AASHTO's Innovation Initiative on California's wrong-way work reported that two-way red and clear retroreflective raised pavement markers were associated with a 44 percent reduction in California Highway Patrol-reported wrong-way events across 60 exit ramps. LED-illuminated signs were associated with a 60 percent reduction on five ramps, and active detection systems with a 53 percent reduction per year on six ramps.

Those are results from specific ramps in one state, and they should be read that way. But the direction is consistent: the more clearly a ramp communicates "not this way," the fewer drivers go the wrong way on it.

Signs you are heading the wrong way

Beyond the official devices, the road gives other clues:

  • You see the backs of signs. Signs are oriented toward traffic. Blank metal facing you is a warning.
  • Pavement arrows point at you. If the arrow on the road points toward your car, you are going against it.
  • Parked or stopped cars face you. On a one-way street or ramp, vehicles should face the same way you do.
  • Headlights are coming at you in your lane. On a divided highway, oncoming headlights should be on the other side of a barrier or median.

What to do if they are facing you

Stop. Do not try to turn around on a ramp or freeway with traffic coming. Pull as far to the edge as you can, turn on your hazard lights, keep your headlights on, stay buckled in the vehicle, and call 911.

Why we write about signs

Road design is one of the most effective tools against wrong-way crashes because it works on every driver, including the ones who never read a safety article. Raising awareness of what these devices mean is a small addition on top of that.

NMWWD supports this kind of practical, layered prevention. If you know of a ramp where the signs are faded, blocked, or confusing, report it to ADOT or your city. And if you would like to help us keep this work going, get involved or donate.
