Almost everything we write about is prevention: avoiding impairment, reading signs, driving in daylight, knowing what to do if headlights come at you. All of that is meant to keep a crash from happening.

The seat belt is what matters when prevention fails. It does not stop a wrong-way driver or an impaired driver from crossing into your path. It is what decides, more than almost anything else inside the car, whether you survive when they do.

With Thanksgiving travel starting next week, it is worth a reminder.

## What the numbers say

NHTSA's figures on seat belts are some of the clearest in traffic safety:

- **48% of passenger vehicle occupants killed in 2024 were unrestrained.**
- **At night, the share was even higher: 56% of those killed during nighttime hours in 2024 were unrestrained.**
- **Seat belts saved an estimated 14,955 lives in 2017.**
- For front-seat passenger car occupants, NHTSA cites research showing seat belts reduce the risk of fatal injury by 45%.

National seat belt use reached 91.3% in 2025. That is high, but the smaller group who do not buckle up make up a hugely disproportionate share of the people who die.

In Arizona, ADOT's 2024 crash facts report counted 340 people killed and 3,088 injured who were not using a seat belt or helmet.

## Why it matters more in the crashes we focus on

Wrong-way crashes are usually head-on crashes at highway speed, which is why they are so much deadlier than most other crash types. The forces in a head-on collision are extreme. A belt keeps occupants in their seats, in position for the airbags to work, and inside the vehicle.

The nighttime numbers matter too. NHTSA's data shows unbuckled occupants are an even larger share of nighttime deaths, and night is when most wrong-way crashes happen. ADOT reports that three out of four occur between 6 p.m. and 6 a.m.

The seat belt also matters in one of the most important pieces of wrong-way advice. If you ever realize you are the one going the wrong way, the guidance is to pull to the edge, stop, turn on your hazard lights, and stay in the vehicle with your seat belt on while you call 911.

## What Arizona law requires

Arizona's seat belt law, A.R.S. 28-909, requires front-seat occupants to wear a seat belt while the vehicle is moving, and requires drivers to make sure passengers under 16 are buckled.

Under the law, it is a secondary offense, which means an officer cannot stop a driver only for a seat belt violation. The maximum civil penalty is $10.

Arizona's child restraint law, A.R.S. 28-907, requires child restraint systems for children under 5, and for children ages 5 through 7 who are not more than 4 feet 9 inches tall.

The low fine and secondary enforcement mean the law does not do much of the work here. Habit has to.

## Habits worth building before the holidays

**Every seat, every trip.** Adults in the back seat are not required by Arizona law to buckle up, but physics does not care which row you are in. An unbuckled rear passenger can also be thrown forward into the people in front.

**The driver sets the rule.** Do not start the car until everyone is buckled. Kids learn this best when it is never negotiable.

**Wear it correctly.** The lap belt goes low across the hips, and the shoulder belt crosses the chest and collarbone, never behind the back or under the arm.

**Check car seats before a long trip.** Make sure children are in the right seat for their size and that the seat is installed correctly. If you are not sure, a certified child passenger safety technician can check it for you.

**Stay buckled on short trips.** The drive to a neighbor's house for a holiday meal is still a drive.

**Stay buckled when you stop on the freeway.** If you break down or are in a minor crash on a highway shoulder, staying in your vehicle with your belt on is generally safer than standing outside next to traffic.

## The one thing in the car you fully control

You cannot control whether an impaired driver gets on the road tonight, or whether someone enters a ramp the wrong way. You can control whether you and your passengers are buckled when you leave the driveway.
