Tomorrow is December 1. December has been recognized as National Impaired Driving Prevention Month, including by presidential proclamation in 2024 and by governors' proclamations in states such as North Carolina in 2025.

The timing makes sense. December brings office parties, family gatherings, New Year's Eve, and long, dark nights. Over and over this month, people will face the same decision about how to get home.

## The numbers behind the month

NHTSA reports that 11,904 people died in alcohol-impaired driving crashes in the United States in 2024. That is one person every 44 minutes, and about 30% of all traffic deaths.

December carries its share of that loss. NHTSA reports that in the combined Decembers of 2020 through 2024, nearly 5,000 people were killed in crashes involving an alcohol-impaired driver, an average of more than 30 lives every day of the holiday season. In December 2024 alone, there were 934 alcohol-impaired driving deaths.

In Arizona, ADOT counted 347 alcohol-related traffic deaths in 2024, about 28% of all traffic deaths in the state.

NHTSA's national "Drive Sober or Get Pulled Over" holiday campaign runs from December 16, 2026, through January 1, 2027, and Arizona agencies typically run holiday DUI enforcement from Thanksgiving through New Year's.

## Why this is the center of our work

NMWWD exists to prevent wrong-way crashes. ADOT reports that two out of three of those crashes are caused by impaired drivers, often with blood alcohol levels more than twice the legal limit.

That makes impaired driving prevention the most direct form of wrong-way prevention there is. Detection systems, illuminated signs, and red reflectors all help after a mistake has started. A sober ride home means the mistake never happens.

## Five ways to make December count

**1. Make your own plan for every event on your calendar.** Office party, neighborhood gathering, New Year's Eve: decide now how you are getting home from each one. Rideshare, a designated driver, or staying over.

**2. Host responsibly.** Serve food and good non-alcoholic choices, stop serving well before the end of the night, and have a couch or guest room ready. Ask early who is driving.

**3. Talk to the people you love.** Teens and young adults home for the holidays, parents and grandparents who take medications, friends who always say they are fine to drive. A short, direct conversation before the season gets busy is worth having.

**4. Speak up in the moment.** If someone is about to drive impaired, offer a ride, call one, or ask them to stay. If they drive anyway, call 911.

**5. Share what to do about a wrong-way driver.** ADOT's guidance is short enough to pass along in a text: if headlights come at you on a divided highway, ease off the gas, steer away when no vehicle is beside you, get to a safe place, and call 911. If an overhead sign warns of a wrong-way driver, move toward the nearest exit as soon as it is safe.

## Supporting the work

Tomorrow is also Giving Tuesday, the day many people set aside for charitable giving after the Thanksgiving weekend. If road safety matters to you, it is a good day to support it.

NMWWD was founded in Phoenix by Nino Mihilli to help prevent wrong-way crashes through awareness, technology, and community. Our work depends on people who care about getting everyone home safely: volunteers who help spread the word, businesses that share safety messages with employees and customers, and donors who make the work possible.

There are a few ways to help this December:

- **Volunteer your time or skills.** If you can help with outreach in your community, or you build software, we would like to hear from you.
- **Bring the message to your workplace, school, or congregation.** Holiday safety reminders are easy to share and reach people right when they matter.
- **Make a gift.** Donations directly support our awareness and technology work.

Visit [our get-involved page](https://nmwwd.org/#involve) to find a way to help, or [make a donation](https://nmwwd.org/#donate) to support safer roads across Arizona this holiday season.
