The night before Thanksgiving has a reputation as one of the biggest nights out of the year. Old friends are back in town, families are reuniting, and many people have the next day off. Bars and restaurants fill up, and plenty of house gatherings start early.

Thanksgiving is tomorrow, November 26. If you are going out tonight, or hosting anyone this weekend, now is the time to decide how everyone is getting home.

## What the holiday weekend looks like on the road

NHTSA's data on the Thanksgiving holiday is sobering:

- **From 2020 through 2024, 910 people died in drunk-driving crashes** during the Thanksgiving holiday period. That was about 36% of all traffic deaths over those holidays.
- **In 2024 alone, 164 people died in drunk-driving crashes** over the Thanksgiving holiday, which NHTSA measures from 6 p.m. Wednesday through 5:59 a.m. the following Monday.
- **Drivers ages 21 to 34** had the highest involvement in fatal drunk-driving crashes on Thanksgiving 2024, at 37%.

That holiday period starts tonight at 6 p.m.

## Why tonight matters for wrong-way risk

ADOT reports that two out of three wrong-way crashes are caused by impaired drivers, often with blood alcohol levels more than twice the legal limit. It also reports that three out of four wrong-way crashes happen between 6 p.m. and 6 a.m., and one-third between midnight and 3 a.m.

A late night out, a crowd of people who have not seen each other in a year, rounds that keep coming, and a drive home after midnight: that is the setting where wrong-way crashes start.

## Make the plan now

The time to decide how you are getting home is before the first drink, while your judgment is fully intact.

**Pick a sober driver, and mean it.** A designated driver is someone who does not drink at all, not someone who plans to have "just a couple."

**Set up your rideshare app before you leave.** Make sure it is updated and your payment works, so it takes a minute to request a ride later instead of ten.

**Leave the car at home** if you think you might drink. It removes the decision entirely.

**Know where you will sleep** if the night runs long. A friend's couch is better than any drive home impaired.

**Agree on it as a group.** It is much easier to stick to the plan when everyone made it together.

## If you are hosting this weekend

Thanksgiving Day itself often involves drinks with the meal and long afternoons of football. Hosts can help keep the drive home safe:

- Serve plenty of food and offer good non-alcoholic options.
- Stop serving alcohol well before guests start to leave.
- Ask early in the evening who is driving.
- Have a guest room or couch ready, and offer it without making it awkward.
- If someone who has been drinking reaches for their keys, step in.

## Looking out for each other

Some of the most effective prevention is not technology or enforcement. It is a friend saying, "Let me get you a ride."

If someone you are with is about to drive impaired:

- Offer to drive them, if you are sober.
- Call them a ride, and wait with them until it arrives.
- Ask them to stay.
- If they insist on driving and you cannot stop them, call 911 and report it.

## On the road tonight

Even if you are sober, you will share the road with people who are not. Late tonight and early tomorrow morning, a few habits help:

- Stay alert, and keep extra following distance.
- Watch the road far ahead, not just the car in front of you, so you notice anything unusual early.
- If you see headlights coming toward you on a divided highway, ADOT advises easing off the gas, steering away when no vehicle is beside you, getting to a safe place, and calling 911.

## The best tradition

Thanksgiving is about the people at the table. The best way to honor that is to make sure every one of them gets there, and gets home.
