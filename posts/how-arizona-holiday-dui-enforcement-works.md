Every year around Thanksgiving, police agencies across Arizona shift into a familiar pattern. Extra officers go out on weekend nights, agencies that normally patrol separately coordinate, and DUI enforcement becomes one of the most visible things law enforcement does until the new year.

Here is what that effort looks like, and why it is worth understanding even if you never plan to drive impaired.

## Why the holidays get special attention

The holiday season combines more parties, more travel, and longer nights. National data shows the cost.

NHTSA reports that in the combined Decembers of 2020 through 2024, nearly 5,000 people were killed in crashes involving an alcohol-impaired driver, an average of more than 30 lives lost each day of the holiday season. In December 2024 alone, there were 934 alcohol-impaired driving deaths.

Thanksgiving is part of the same pattern. NHTSA reports that from 2020 through 2024, 910 people died in drunk-driving crashes during the Thanksgiving holiday period, about 36% of all traffic deaths during those holidays.

In Arizona, ADOT counted 347 alcohol-related traffic deaths in 2024, and 2,407 alcohol-related crashes on nights and weekends that year.

## What a holiday task force is

A holiday DUI task force is a coordinated enforcement effort among multiple agencies. Rather than each department working its own city on its own schedule, officers from several agencies concentrate on the same nights and areas.

The City of Scottsdale's description of its 2024 holiday season task force gives a sense of the scale. Scottsdale police led the effort with the Governor's Office of Highway Safety, ADOT, and NHTSA, and partner agencies included the Arizona Department of Public Safety, the Maricopa County Sheriff's Office, and police departments in Phoenix, Mesa, Chandler, Gilbert, Tempe, Apache Junction, Queen Creek, Salt River, and ASU.

The task force worked evening and late-night hours on weekends from the Friday after Thanksgiving through the morning of New Year's Day, using saturation patrols that rotated among command post locations.

Agencies in other parts of the state run their own holiday enforcement efforts as well.

## What the numbers look like

Holiday enforcement in Arizona produces large numbers of arrests. ABC15 reported in December 2024 that Arizona's holiday DUI task force had made more than 1,500 DUI arrests between Thanksgiving and December 16 that year, an average of more than 80 a day.

Each of those arrests is a driver taken off the road before something worse happened.

## What saturation patrols are

A saturation patrol puts a concentrated number of officers in one area for a set period, watching for traffic violations and driving behavior that can signal impairment. When an officer stops a driver and sees signs of impairment, that can lead to further testing and, if warranted, an arrest.

The goal is both to catch impaired drivers and to make enforcement visible enough that people decide not to drive impaired in the first place.

## Why enforcement matters for wrong-way crashes

ADOT reports that two out of three wrong-way crashes are caused by impaired drivers, often with blood alcohol levels more than twice the legal limit. Three out of four happen between 6 p.m. and 6 a.m., and more than half on weekends.

Those are the same nights and hours that holiday task forces target. An impaired driver stopped for weaving on a surface street at 11 p.m. is a driver who will not be entering a freeway off-ramp at 1 a.m.

Enforcement is not the whole answer. It can only reach a small fraction of the drivers on the road at any time. But it does two things well: it removes some of the most dangerous drivers from the road on the most dangerous nights, and it reminds everyone else that the risk of getting caught is real.

## The better outcome

Nobody involved in holiday DUI enforcement wants to make more arrests. The best outcome is a season where officers find fewer impaired drivers because more people planned ahead.

Scottsdale's announcement put it simply: DUIs are 100 percent preventable. If you plan to drink or use recreational marijuana, plan a sober designated driver, rideshare, or taxi before you go.

## How you can help

- **Plan your ride before the first drink.**
- **Speak up** if someone you know is about to drive impaired.
- **Call 911** if you see a driver who appears impaired or is going the wrong way, once you are safely able to.
- **Share these basics** with family and friends before the holiday parties start.
